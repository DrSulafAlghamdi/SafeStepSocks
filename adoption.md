# Adoption and sustainability — proposed strategy

## Target users and buyers

- Adults with elevated fall risk and caregivers (consumer channel).
- Home-healthcare services, physiotherapy and rehabilitation groups (clinical and professional channels).
- Hospitals, long-term-care organizations and assisted-living providers (institutional channel).

## Prospective Saudi organizations to approach (not partners)

- **Saudi German Health:** assess interest in clinician-guided usability or home-care pilot programs.
- **Fakeeh Care:** assess rehabilitation and home-healthcare fit.
- **Dr. Sulaiman Al Habib Medical Group:** evaluate institutional clinical pilot opportunities.
- **NUPCO:** investigate public-sector supplier and medical-device procurement requirements after appropriate approvals.

These are **possible outreach targets, not adopters, endorsements, customers or signed collaborators**. Confirm procurement criteria directly with each organization.

## Proposed partnership pathway

1. Seek formal written clinical feedback and a feasibility review.
2. Obtain a non-binding letter of interest, with approved wording for communications.
3. Agree a protocol and clinical/ethics oversight before user testing.
4. Demonstrate accuracy, safety, wash durability and total cost of ownership.
5. Meet applicable Saudi medical-device, data-protection and procurement requirements before sales.

## Sustainability considerations

- Modular reusable electronics could reduce component disposal compared with fully disposable electronics, but requires lifecycle study.
- Estimate BOM, assembly labor, return rates, textile replacement frequency, packaging and battery life.
- Explore direct consumer sales, institutional supply and optional support plans without making untested subscription assumptions.
- Prioritize accessible sizing, comfort and safe wear; do not guarantee falls will be prevented.
